The old bookstore downstairs: I passed it for three years and never once went in.
The owner was a grey-haired old man who always sat in a rattan chair at the door with a book open on his knees, never greeting customers on his own.

Last week it poured, so I ducked in to shelter from the rain.
The shop was darker than I had imagined, shelves reaching the ceiling, the air thick with the smell of damp paper.
The old man looked up at me, said nothing, and got up to pour me a cup of hot water.

I pulled a book off the shelf at random and opened the first page; a yellowed movie ticket stub was pressed inside.
The old man said the books were all sold to him by other people, and he never took out what was left inside them.
He said it belonged to the original owner, not to him.

When the rain stopped, I bought that book.
Stepping outside, I suddenly realized I had lived on this street for three years and had never really looked at it.

We think we know everything around us.
But often, we have only walked past it.
